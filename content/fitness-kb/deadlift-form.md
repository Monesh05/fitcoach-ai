---
title: Conventional Deadlift Form Checklist
category: exercise-form
---

The deadlift is a hip-hinge movement and one of the most effective exercises for the posterior chain (glutes, hamstrings, spinal erectors) and overall strength. Key form checkpoints, useful for coaching cues and evaluating a submitted photo/video:

Setup:
- Bar over mid-foot, close to the shins.
- Feet roughly hip-width apart, grip just outside the legs.
- Hips higher than the knees but lower than the shoulders at the start — shins should touch or nearly touch the bar.
- Neutral spine: chest up, back flat (not rounded, not overly arched), core braced.

Pull:
- Take the slack out of the bar before pulling explosively — the bar should not "jump" off the floor.
- Push the floor away with the legs while keeping the bar close to the body (dragging up the shins/thighs).
- Hips and shoulders should rise at the same rate — hips shooting up first ("stripper deadlift") indicates the legs are disengaging too early and turns it into a stiff-legged pull.
- Lock out by extending the hips fully; avoid excessive lower-back hyperextension/leaning back at the top.

Descent:
- Push the hips back first, then bend the knees as the bar passes them, reversing the setup position — don't just drop the bar or squat it down.

Common faults to flag when reviewing a photo/video:
1. Rounded lower back at the start or during the pull — the single most injury-relevant fault; cue "chest up, brace before you pull," reduce load until form is fixed.
2. Bar drifting away from the body (visible gap between bar and shins/thighs) — increases lower-back strain and reduces leverage.
3. Hips rising too fast relative to the chest — cue "push the floor away," check quad strength/positioning.
4. Hyperextending (leaning back) at lockout — cue "stand up tall, don't lean back."

Note: some rounding of the upper back (thoracic spine) is normal and not the same safety concern as rounding of the lower back (lumbar spine) — the two should be evaluated separately.
