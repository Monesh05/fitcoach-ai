---
title: Meal Timing and Nutrient Timing
category: nutrition
---

Total daily calories and protein matter far more for body composition than the precise timing of meals — but timing can offer smaller, secondary benefits for performance, recovery, and adherence.

**Pre-workout:**
- A meal with carbs and moderate protein 1-3 hours before training generally supports better performance than training fully fasted, especially for longer or higher-intensity sessions.
- Training fasted is not harmful for most healthy people and doesn't meaningfully change fat loss outcomes over time, but many people perform worse in the gym without any pre-workout fuel.

**Post-workout ("anabolic window"):**
- The idea of a narrow 30-60 minute post-workout window where protein must be consumed to avoid missing out on muscle gains is outdated — research shows this window is much wider (several hours) than once believed, especially if a pre-workout meal was eaten.
- That said, eating a protein-containing meal within a few hours of training is a reasonable, low-effort practice, and matters more when training in a fasted state or with long gaps between meals.

**Meal frequency:**
- Eating 3 meals vs. 5-6 smaller meals per day produces similar body composition outcomes at equal total calories and protein — meal frequency is mainly a matter of personal preference, hunger management, and lifestyle fit, not a distinct metabolic advantage.
- Spreading protein across 3-4+ meals (roughly 0.3-0.5 g/kg per meal) does appear to support muscle protein synthesis slightly better than consuming it all in 1-2 large meals, though this is a smaller effect than total daily protein.

**Practical takeaway:** get total daily calories, protein, and training consistency right first — then use timing (eating before/after training, spreading protein across meals) as a secondary optimization, not the primary lever.
