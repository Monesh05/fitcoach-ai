---
title: A Practical Framework for Beginners
category: training-principles
---

Someone new to structured resistance training has the highest potential rate of progress they will ever have ("newbie gains") — the priority is building consistent habits and technique, not optimizing every variable.

**Program structure for beginners:**
- Full-body workouts 2-4x/week (or an upper/lower split 4x/week) covering the main movement patterns: squat, hinge, horizontal push, horizontal pull, vertical push, vertical pull, and some core work.
- 2-4 sets per exercise, in the 5-15 rep range, is a broadly effective range while learning technique — very heavy 1-3 rep work is lower priority until technique is solid.
- Linear progression (adding a small amount of weight each session or week on the main lifts) works very well for the first several months, since almost any reasonable, consistent stimulus produces adaptation at this stage.

**Priorities, in rough order:**
1. Consistency — showing up 2-4x/week reliably matters more than program "optimality."
2. Technique — learning to perform the main compound lifts safely and effectively before chasing heavy weight.
3. Progressive overload — a simple, trackable way to add difficulty over time (see progressive overload guidance).
4. Recovery basics — adequate sleep and protein intake to support adaptation.
5. Fine-tuning volume/intensity/exercise selection — this becomes relevant later, once "newbie gains" slow down (typically after 6-12 months of consistent training).

**Common beginner mistakes to watch for:**
- Program-hopping every few weeks before giving a program enough time to work (a program needs at least 6-12 weeks of honest effort to judge).
- Chasing heavy weight with poor technique before building a base of movement quality — increases injury risk and often ingrains bad habits that are hard to unlearn later.
- Neglecting nutrition (especially protein and total calories) while expecting training alone to produce results.
- Excessive exercise variety/volume in early sessions leading to extreme soreness that discourages continued training — starting conservatively and progressing volume gradually over the first few weeks is more sustainable.
