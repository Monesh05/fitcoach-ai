---
title: Barbell Bench Press Form Checklist
category: exercise-form
---

The bench press is the primary horizontal pressing movement for chest, shoulders, and triceps. Key form checkpoints:

Setup:
- Lie back so the eyes are roughly under the bar.
- Shoulder blades retracted and depressed ("pulled back and down") to create a stable base and protect the shoulders.
- Feet flat on the floor, a slight arch in the lower back is normal and helps stability — this is different from excessive, unstable arching.
- Grip slightly wider than shoulder width; forearms should be roughly vertical at the bottom of the movement.

Descent:
- Lower the bar under control to the mid-to-lower chest (roughly nipple line), not the neck or upper chest, for most lifters.
- Elbows tucked at a moderate angle (commonly ~45-75 degrees from the torso) rather than flared straight out to the sides, which stresses the shoulder joint more.

Press:
- Drive the bar up and slightly back toward the face in a slight arc, keeping shoulder blades retracted throughout (don't let them roll forward as you press).
- Keep feet planted and drive through the legs for stability (leg drive), without lifting the hips off the bench.

Common faults to flag when reviewing a photo/video:
1. Flared elbows (close to 90 degrees from the torso) — increases shoulder strain; cue "tuck the elbows slightly."
2. Bouncing the bar off the chest — reduces control and increases injury risk; cue "controlled touch, then press."
3. Shoulders rolling forward off the bench at the bottom — reduces stability and shoulder protection; cue "keep the chest up, shoulder blades pinned."
4. Uneven bar path (one side lower/higher) — often indicates a strength imbalance between sides; consider unilateral accessory work (e.g. single-arm dumbbell press).

Always use a spotter or safety bars when attempting a near-maximal bench press.
