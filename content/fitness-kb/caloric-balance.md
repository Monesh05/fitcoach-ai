---
title: Caloric Balance for Fat Loss and Muscle Gain
category: nutrition
---

Body weight change is governed primarily by energy balance: calories consumed versus calories expended (basal metabolism + activity + exercise + digestion).

Fat loss (caloric deficit):
- A deficit of roughly 300-500 kcal/day below maintenance typically produces ~0.25-0.5 kg (0.5-1 lb) of fat loss per week, a sustainable pace that helps preserve muscle mass.
- Very aggressive deficits (>25-30% below maintenance) increase muscle loss risk, fatigue, and adherence failure, and are generally not recommended for extended periods without supervision.
- Estimate maintenance calories using bodyweight, activity level, and tools like the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then adjust based on real-world weight trend over 2-3 weeks rather than a single day's number.

Muscle gain (caloric surplus):
- A modest surplus of roughly 200-300 kcal/day above maintenance supports muscle growth while limiting excess fat gain, especially for intermediate/advanced lifters whose muscle-building potential per month is lower than beginners'.
- Beginners ("newbie gains") can often build muscle even at maintenance or a slight deficit due to high initial adaptation potential, especially if new to structured resistance training.

General notes:
- Weight fluctuates day to day due to water, sodium, and digestion; track weekly averages, not daily numbers.
- Protein and resistance training are the two biggest levers for preserving/building muscle regardless of whether the goal is fat loss or gain.
