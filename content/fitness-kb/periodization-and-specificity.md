---
title: Periodization and the Principle of Specificity
category: training-principles
---

Specificity means the body adapts to the specific demands placed on it — training for maximal strength (low reps, heavy loads) produces different adaptations than training for muscular endurance (high reps, lighter loads), even though both involve resistance training. Programs should match exercise selection, rep ranges, and rest periods to the actual goal.

Periodization is the deliberate, planned variation of training volume and intensity over time to manage fatigue and continue driving adaptation:

- **Linear periodization**: volume starts high and intensity low, then over weeks/months volume decreases as intensity (load) increases, typically building toward a strength peak. Common in beginner-to-intermediate strength programs.
- **Undulating periodization**: volume and intensity vary session-to-session or week-to-week (e.g. a heavy day, a moderate day, a light/high-rep day within the same week) rather than a single long progression. Often better suited to intermediate/advanced lifters training a lift frequently.
- **Block periodization**: distinct blocks of training (e.g. a hypertrophy block, then a strength block, then a peaking block) each with a different primary focus, common in advanced/competitive programming.

Practical guidance:
- Beginners generally do well with simple linear progression on compound lifts and don't need complex periodization schemes.
- Intermediate lifters, whose rate of progress has slowed, benefit from planned variation (undulating periodization or periodic deloads) to keep progressing without accumulating excessive fatigue.
- Regardless of the periodization model used, progress should still be tracked (weights, reps, sets) so decisions are based on real performance data rather than guesswork.
