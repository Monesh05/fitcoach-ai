---
title: Barbell Back Squat Form Checklist
category: exercise-form
---

The back squat is a foundational compound lift for lower body strength. Common form checkpoints, useful both for coaching cues and for evaluating a user-submitted photo/video of their squat:

Setup:
- Bar rests on the upper traps (high-bar) or rear delts (low-bar), not on the neck.
- Feet roughly shoulder-width apart, toes turned out slightly (10-30 degrees).
- Core braced (a deep breath into the belly, not just the chest) before descending.

Descent:
- Hips and knees bend together — avoid initiating with the knees only (knees caving too far forward early) or the hips only (excessive forward lean too early).
- Knees track in line with the toes; valgus collapse (knees caving inward) is a common fault linked to weak hip abductors/glute medius.
- Descend to at least parallel (hip crease at or below knee level) if mobility allows, maintaining a neutral spine (no excessive rounding of the lower back, known as "butt wink," at the bottom).

Ascent:
- Drive through the whole foot (mid-foot to heel), not just the toes.
- Hips and shoulders should rise at roughly the same rate — hips shooting up much faster than the chest ("good morning squat") indicates weak quads relative to posterior chain or poor bracing.
- Knees continue to track over the toes throughout the ascent.

Common faults to flag when reviewing a photo/video:
1. Knee valgus (knees caving in) — cue: "push knees out," strengthen hip abductors.
2. Excessive forward lean / rounded lower back — cue: brace core harder, check ankle mobility, check bar position.
3. Heels rising off the floor — often an ankle mobility limitation; consider a slight heel-elevated shoe or box squats to a safe depth while mobility improves.
4. Not reaching depth — could be intentional (partial-range training) or a mobility/stability limitation; note it rather than assume it's wrong.
