---
title: Warm-Up and Injury Prevention
category: safety
---

A structured warm-up prepares the muscles, joints, and nervous system for the specific demands of a workout, and is one of the most effective low-cost ways to reduce injury risk.

Recommended warm-up structure:
1. General warm-up (3-5 minutes): light cardio (e.g. brisk walk, bike, rowing) to raise heart rate and core temperature.
2. Dynamic mobility (3-5 minutes): movement-based stretches relevant to the session (e.g. leg swings and hip circles before squats; band pull-aparts and arm circles before pressing).
3. Movement-specific ramp-up sets: for a compound lift like the squat or bench press, perform several progressively heavier warm-up sets (e.g. bar only, then ~50%, ~70%, ~85% of working weight) before the first working set, rather than jumping straight to a heavy load.

Injury-prevention principles:
- Progress load and volume gradually (see progressive overload guidance) — sudden large jumps in weight, volume, or a new/unfamiliar movement pattern are common injury triggers.
- Maintain full, controlled range of motion with good technique rather than sacrificing form to lift heavier or move faster.
- Address persistent pain (sharp, joint-related, or pain that worsens during a movement) by stopping the movement and, if it persists, consulting a medical professional — this is different from normal muscular fatigue/soreness (DOMS).
- Static stretching (holding a stretch) is generally better placed after training or on rest days rather than immediately before heavy lifting, since some evidence suggests it can temporarily reduce maximal force output when done right before a max-effort lift.

This is general safety guidance and is not a substitute for individualized medical or physical therapy advice, especially for anyone with an existing injury.
