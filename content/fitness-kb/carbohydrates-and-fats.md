---
title: Carbohydrates and Dietary Fats for Training
category: nutrition
---

After total calories and protein, carbohydrates and fats are the remaining macronutrients that fill out a nutrition plan and support training performance.

**Carbohydrates:**
- Carbs are the body's preferred fuel for moderate-to-high intensity exercise (weight training, sprinting, team sports); they're stored as glycogen in muscles and the liver.
- General guidance for active individuals: roughly 3-5 g/kg bodyweight/day for moderate training loads, up to 5-8+ g/kg/day for high-volume endurance athletes. Strength/hypertrophy-focused lifters doing moderate cardio typically land in the 3-5 g/kg range, adjusted based on total calorie targets.
- Low-carb approaches can still support muscle building/fat loss for some people, but very low carb intake often reduces training performance and recovery capacity for glycolytic (moderate-to-high rep resistance training) activities.
- Good sources: rice, oats, potatoes, fruit, whole grains, legumes — prioritizing minimally processed sources most of the time supports fiber intake and satiety.

**Dietary fats:**
- Fats support hormone production (including testosterone), cell membrane health, and fat-soluble vitamin absorption (A, D, E, K).
- General guidance: roughly 0.5-1.0 g/kg bodyweight/day, or about 20-35% of total daily calories, is a reasonable range for most people; going much lower than ~15-20% of calories long-term can impair hormone production.
- Prioritize unsaturated fats (olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado, fatty fish) most of the time, while saturated fat (fatty meat, butter, ghee, coconut oil) in moderation is generally fine within an otherwise balanced diet for most healthy individuals.

**Filling out a daily target:** once daily calories and protein are set, remaining calories are typically split between carbs and fat based on training demands, personal preference, and how the individual feels/performs — there is no single universally "correct" carb-to-fat ratio as long as protein, total calories, and micronutrient needs are met.
