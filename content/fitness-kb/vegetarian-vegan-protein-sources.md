---
title: Vegetarian and Vegan Protein Sources
category: nutrition
---

Building muscle on a vegetarian or vegan diet is entirely achievable — it just requires slightly more deliberate planning around protein quantity, variety, and amino acid completeness than an omnivorous diet.

**Complete vs. incomplete proteins:** animal proteins (dairy, eggs, meat, fish) and soy are "complete," containing all nine essential amino acids in ample amounts. Most plant proteins (legumes, grains, nuts) are lower in one or more essential amino acids (commonly lysine in grains, methionine in legumes) — combining different plant sources across the day (not necessarily the same meal) covers this easily.

**Vegetarian protein sources** (include dairy/eggs): eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese/paneer, milk, whey protein, plus all vegan sources below.

**Vegan protein sources:**
- Legumes: lentils, chickpeas, black beans, kidney beans — roughly 15-18g protein per cooked cup.
- Soy products: tofu, tempeh, edamame, soy milk, soy chunks — soy protein is a complete protein and among the highest-quality plant proteins.
- Seitan (wheat gluten) — very high protein by weight, though low in lysine; pair with legumes.
- Quinoa — one of the few grains that is a complete protein on its own, though lower in total protein density than legumes.
- Nuts and seeds (peanuts, almonds, hemp seeds, chia, pumpkin seeds) — moderate protein, useful as an addition rather than a primary source due to higher calorie density.
- Plant-based protein powders (pea, rice, soy, or blends) — useful for conveniently hitting protein targets, especially post-workout or when whole-food options are impractical.

**Practical guidance for vegetarians/vegans building muscle:**
- Since plant proteins are often slightly less "anabolically efficient" per gram than animal/whey protein (due to amino acid profile and digestibility), aiming for the higher end of protein recommendations (~1.8-2.2 g/kg bodyweight/day) is a reasonable adjustment.
- Spread protein across meals and include a variety of sources (legumes + grains + soy) rather than relying on a single source.
- Vegans in particular should also monitor vitamin B12 (found almost exclusively in animal products or fortified foods/supplements), iron, zinc, and omega-3s, which are more work to obtain adequately on a fully plant-based diet — a basic multivitamin or B12 supplement is commonly recommended for vegans.
