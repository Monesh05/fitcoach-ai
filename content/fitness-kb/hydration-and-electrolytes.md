---
title: Hydration and Electrolytes for Training
category: nutrition
---

Even mild dehydration (as little as 2% of bodyweight lost as fluid) can measurably reduce strength, endurance, and cognitive performance during training.

General guidance:
- A common baseline recommendation is roughly 30-40 ml of water per kg of bodyweight per day (e.g. ~2.1-2.8 L for a 70kg person) from all fluids and food, adjusted upward for hot climates, high sweat rates, or higher body sizes.
- On training days, an additional 500ml-1L (or more, for long/hot sessions) around the workout helps offset sweat losses.
- Thirst is a reasonably reliable short-term signal for most people; consistently pale-yellow urine is a simple practical indicator of adequate hydration, while dark urine suggests under-hydration.

Electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium):
- Sodium is lost in sweat and is important for fluid balance, nerve function, and muscle contraction; athletes training heavily in heat or for long durations (60+ minutes with heavy sweating) may benefit from added sodium (e.g. an electrolyte drink or adding salt to meals) beyond what a normal diet provides.
- For most moderate-duration resistance training sessions (under an hour, indoor/climate-controlled), plain water is usually sufficient and specialized electrolyte drinks aren't necessary.
- Potassium and magnesium needs are generally well covered by a varied diet including fruits, vegetables, legumes, nuts, and whole grains.

Practical notes:
- Overhydration (excessive water intake without adequate sodium, "water intoxication") is rare but possible in endurance events lasting several hours; it is not a practical concern for typical gym-goers.
- Caffeinated beverages (coffee, tea) do have a mild diuretic effect but contribute meaningfully to daily fluid intake and don't need to be excluded when counting hydration.
