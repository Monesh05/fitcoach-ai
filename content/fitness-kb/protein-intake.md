---
title: Protein Intake for Muscle Growth and Recovery
category: nutrition
---

Protein provides the amino acids needed to repair and build muscle tissue after training, and adequate intake is one of the most important nutrition variables for anyone strength training.

General guidance (for healthy adults without kidney disease):
- Muscle building / strength training: roughly 1.6-2.2 g of protein per kg of bodyweight per day (about 0.7-1.0 g per lb).
- General health / maintenance without heavy training: roughly 0.8-1.2 g per kg bodyweight per day.
- During a calorie deficit (fat loss), protein needs trend toward the higher end of the range (up to ~2.2-2.4 g/kg) to help preserve lean muscle mass.

Distribution and sources:
- Spreading protein across 3-4 meals (roughly 0.3-0.5 g/kg per meal) appears to support muscle protein synthesis better than consuming it all in one meal.
- Complete protein sources (containing all essential amino acids) include eggs, dairy, meat, fish, poultry, and soy. Combining plant sources (e.g. rice + beans, or various legumes + grains) across the day can also meet essential amino acid needs for those eating plant-based.
- Post-workout protein timing matters less than total daily intake for most people — consistency across the day and week is the bigger lever.

Caveats: individuals with pre-existing kidney disease should consult a physician before significantly increasing protein intake. These are general population guidelines, not medical advice.
